Maybe you have been ill, at home with small children, caring for a parent, or looking for work for longer than you hoped. Maybe you also moved to Norway in the middle of it all. A gap of one, two or five years on your CV can feel like a big problem. In practice, a career break is something employers see often. What matters is how clearly and calmly you explain it, and what you show you can do now.

This guide shows you how to handle a career break in your CV, your job application (søknad) and the interview when you apply for jobs in Norway.

## Be honest and brief

Norwegian application culture values honesty and modesty. A short, direct explanation usually works better than a long defence or a hidden gap. Recruiters notice missing years, and an unexplained gap invites guessing.

A few principles to keep in mind:

- **Name the break in one line.** You do not need to tell the whole story.
- **Keep private details private.** If you were ill, you do not need to name the illness. Saying you were on sick leave and are now ready to work is enough.
- **Point forward.** Spend most of your words on what you can offer now, not on why you were away.
- **Do not apologise.** Words like *unfortunately* or *I know this looks bad* weaken an otherwise solid application.

## How to show the break in your CV

A Norwegian CV is usually written in reverse chronological order and is one to two pages long. Treat your career break as its own entry, with dates, just like a job. This shows you are open about it and stops the reader from searching for the gap.

Here is an example from an invented CV:

> **2022–2024** Career break: full-time care for a family member
> **2017–2022** Customer service adviser, Y AS, Bergen
> **2014–2017** Sales assistant, a supermarket, Trondheim

If you did something useful during the break, add one or two bullet points under it:

> **2021–2023** Parental leave and relocation to Norway
> - Norwegian course, level A2 to B1
> - Volunteer at a local sports club, organising match-day kiosks

A few practical tips:

- Use years only, or month and year, but be consistent through the whole CV.
- If your skills are stronger than your recent job titles, put a short **profile** or **key skills** section at the top, so the reader sees your strengths before the dates.
- References are commonly listed with name, role and phone number, or written as *available on request*. A former manager from before the break is still a good reference. Call them first and tell them you are applying again.

## Wording that works for different reasons

You can adapt these example lines for your CV or søknad. Keep them short.

**Illness or sick leave**

> I was away from work for a period due to health reasons. I am now fully ready to return and have been preparing through an online course in Excel.

**Parental leave**

> After two years at home with my children, I am ready to return to full-time work and keen to use my experience in logistics again.

**Caring for a family member**

> From 2022 to 2024 I cared for a close family member. The situation is now resolved, and I can commit fully to a new position.

**Unemployment**

> Since my last contract ended, I have used the time to improve my Norwegian and complete a forklift course, and I have worked as a volunteer at a second-hand shop.

**Moving to Norway**

> After moving to Norway in 2023, I focused on learning Norwegian and understanding the local job market. I now speak conversational Norwegian (B1) and am taking an evening course to reach B2.

## Show what you did during the break

Many people do more during a break than they give themselves credit for. Look for anything that shows you stayed active, kept learning or used real skills:

- Language courses, especially Norwegian
- Online or evening courses related to your field
- Volunteering, for example at a local sports club, a church or a parents' committee at your child's school
- Organising, budgeting or planning, such as managing care appointments or running a household move abroad
- Short or part-time jobs, even outside your field

Many job ads (stillingsannonser) in Norway ask for Norwegian skills. Describe your level honestly. If you know your CEFR level, use it, such as *Norwegian: B1*. If not, use plain words like *basic*, *conversational* or *fluent*, and add what you are doing to improve. A break spent learning Norwegian is a strong, concrete story.

## Mention the break in your søknad, then move on

The Norwegian søknad is usually no longer than one page and is addressed to the named contact person if the ad gives one. Give the career break one or two sentences, ideally in the middle, not in your opening line. Open with why you want this job.

**Before:**

> I am sorry that I have not worked for three years. It has been a very hard time for my family, and I hope you will still consider me.

**After:**

> I have spent the last three years caring for a family member, and I am now ready to return to work. During this time I kept my skills up to date through a course in bookkeeping, which I see as directly useful for this role at Y AS.

The second version is honest, short and links the break to the job. For a full structure, see [what to write in a Norwegian cover letter, paragraph by paragraph](/en/norwegian-cover-letter/).

## Choose where to start

You can find job ads on [FINN.no](https://www.finn.no/job), [arbeidsplassen.no](https://arbeidsplassen.nav.no) run by NAV, and [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com). Always check the application deadline (søknadsfrist) in each ad.

After a long break, it can help to look at:

- Jobs close to what you did before, where your earlier experience counts most
- Part-time or temporary positions that can lead to something more permanent
- Roles where your break gave you relevant experience. For example, caring for a parent may make a job at a care home a natural next step.

Read each ad carefully and apply where you meet most of the requirements, rather than sending the same CV everywhere.

## Prepare for the interview question

If your break is on your CV, expect a question about it. Prepare a calm answer of three or four sentences: what happened, what you did during the break, and why you are ready now. Practise it out loud until it sounds natural. Then steer the conversation back to the job. You can find more example answers in [common job interview questions in Norway](/en/job-interview-norway-questions/).

> I took two years off after my son was born. During that time I finished a Norwegian course and helped run the parents' committee at the kindergarten. I am now ready to work full-time, and this role fits my background in warehouse work well.

## Summary

- Show the career break openly in your CV as a dated entry.
- Explain it in one or two sentences, without apologising or sharing private medical details.
- Highlight what you did during the break, such as courses, volunteering and Norwegian lessons.
- In your søknad, open with the job and give the break a short, forward-looking mention.
- Keep references from before the break, and prepare a short interview answer.

### Should I leave the career break out of my CV?

It is usually wiser to include it. A missing period with no explanation makes the reader guess, while one honest line with dates shows openness. Treat it as a normal entry in your reverse chronological CV.

### Do I have to tell the employer what illness I had?

No, you can keep health details private. It is usually enough to say you were away for health reasons and are now ready to return to work. Focus on what you can do in the job now.

### Can I use references from before my career break?

Yes. A former manager or colleague who knows your work is still a valuable reference, even if it was a few years ago. Contact them before you apply so they know to expect a call.

### My Norwegian is not good yet. Should I still apply?

Read the ad carefully. Some jobs require fluent Norwegian, while others accept English or a basic level. Describe your level honestly, for example B1 or conversational, and mention any course you are taking.

### How long should the explanation in my søknad be?

One or two sentences is usually enough. Mention the break briefly, add what you did during it if relevant, and then return to why you fit the job. The søknad should normally stay within one page.
