In Norway, the cover letter is called a *søknad* (job application). It goes with your CV and answers one question for the employer: why are you a good fit for this specific job? A Norwegian søknad is usually no longer than one page. It is direct, honest and modest. Big claims and long life stories tend to work against you.

This guide goes through a søknad part by part, with example sentences you can adapt. The examples are in English, because many employers in Norway accept applications in English. If the ad is written in Norwegian and asks for Norwegian skills, think carefully about which language to use. That is covered below.

## Before you write: read the job ad closely

A good søknad starts with the job ad (*stillingsannonse*), not with a blank page. You will find ads on [FINN.no](https://www.finn.no/job), on [arbeidsplassen.nav.no](https://arbeidsplassen.nav.no) (run by NAV) and on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com). Before you write, note down:

1. The job title and any reference number
2. The contact person (*kontaktperson*), if the ad names one
3. The application deadline (*søknadsfrist*)
4. The three or four most important requirements (*kvalifikasjoner*) and the qualities they want (*personlige egenskaper*)
5. Whether they mention Norwegian language skills

Your søknad should answer the requirements in point 4. If you cannot connect your experience to them, the letter will feel generic.

## The top of the letter: details and greeting

Keep the header simple. Put your name, phone number and email at the top, then the date and the job title. You do not need a full postal address block for the employer.

> Application for the position of Warehouse Assistant, Y AS (ref. 2024-17)

If the ad names a contact person, address them by name. Norwegian workplaces are informal, so first name and surname without a title is normal:

> Dear Ingrid Berg,

If no name is given, use "Dear hiring team" or "Hello". Avoid "To whom it may concern", which feels stiff in Norway.

## Paragraph 1: which job, and why you are writing

The opening should say clearly which position you are applying for and give one short reason why it interests you. Skip phrases such as "I am writing to express my keen interest". Get to the point.

**Before:**

> My name is Amir and I am a hardworking, passionate and dedicated person who has always dreamed of working in logistics.

**After:**

> I am applying for the position of Warehouse Assistant at Y AS, which I found on FINN.no. I have two years of experience in a distribution warehouse, and I am interested in your work with fast deliveries to local shops.

The second version is calmer, more specific and easier to trust. That is the tone Norwegian employers usually respond to.

## Paragraph 2: your most relevant experience

This is the core of the søknad. Pick the two or three requirements from the ad that matter most, and show with concrete examples that you meet them. Do not repeat your whole CV. The CV lists what you have done. The søknad explains why it matters for this job.

> In my previous job at X Logistics, I picked and packed orders with a handheld scanner and was responsible for checking incoming goods. I am used to working to tight deadlines and have a forklift certificate from my home country.

If you have little work experience, use studies, part-time jobs or volunteering instead:

> During my master's studies at Z University, I worked part-time in a supermarket, where I handled the till, restocked shelves and trained two new colleagues.

For more ideas, see [how to write a CV for Norway with no work experience](/en/cv-norway-no-experience/).

## Paragraph 3: why this employer, and how you work

Show that you have read about the company and explain how you would fit in. One or two sentences about the employer is enough. Then describe how you work, with examples rather than adjectives.

**Before:**

> I am a team player with excellent communication skills.

**After:**

> At the care home where I worked as a summer temp, I often covered shifts at short notice and made sure handovers to the next shift were clear and written down.

Norwegian workplaces tend to value cooperation, independence and reliability, but let your examples show these qualities. A modest, factual description is usually more convincing than strong self-praise.

## How to mention your Norwegian language skills

Many ads ask for Norwegian skills. Be honest and specific, and say what you are doing to improve. If you know your level on the CEFR scale (A1–C2), use it. If not, plain words such as "basic", "conversational" or "fluent" work well.

> I speak Norwegian at a conversational level (around B1) and use it daily with colleagues and customers. I am taking an evening course to reach B2.

> My Norwegian is at a basic level. I can handle simple conversations, and I study for one hour every day. My English is fluent.

Never overstate your level. It will become clear in the interview, and it damages trust. If the ad is in Norwegian and you write the søknad in English, a short note on your Norwegian level is especially useful.

## The closing paragraph and sign-off

End briefly. Say that you would welcome an interview, and mention when you can start if that is relevant. Keep the tone polite rather than pushy.

> I would be glad to tell you more in an interview. I can start from 1 March. Thank you for considering my application.

> Kind regards,
> Amir Haddad

You do not need to list references in the søknad. They belong in your CV, with name, role and phone number, or with the line "References available on request" (*referanser oppgis på forespørsel*).

## A quick checklist before you send

- One page or less
- Addressed to the named contact person, if there is one
- The job title and company name are correct (check this carefully if you reuse an old letter)
- Each main requirement in the ad is answered with a concrete example
- Your Norwegian level is described honestly
- Sent before the søknadsfrist

For more pitfalls, read [job application mistakes to avoid in Norway](/en/job-application-norway-mistakes/).

## Summary

A Norwegian søknad is short, specific and honest. Name the job and your reason for applying in the first paragraph. Prove your most relevant experience in the second. Show how you work and why this employer interests you in the third. Describe your Norwegian level truthfully, close politely and keep it all to one page. Write a new version for each job, built around the requirements in that ad.

### Should I write my søknad in Norwegian or English?

If the ad is in English, write in English. If the ad is in Norwegian and asks for Norwegian skills, a søknad in Norwegian can show your level, but only if you can write it well yourself. If your Norwegian is still basic, an English søknad with an honest note about your Norwegian level is usually the better choice.

### How long should a Norwegian cover letter be?

Usually no more than one page. Three or four short paragraphs are enough for most jobs. Employers often read many applications, so a short and specific letter is easier to remember.

### Who do I address the søknad to if no name is given?

Check whether the ad lists a contact person, sometimes near the bottom under kontaktperson. If there is no name, "Dear hiring team" or a simple "Hello" is fine. Avoid "To whom it may concern", which sounds old-fashioned in Norway.

### Can I use the same søknad for several jobs?

You can reuse a basic structure, but each søknad should be adapted to the specific ad. Employers notice generic letters quickly. At least the opening and the experience paragraph should match the requirements of that job.

### Should I mention my background or where I come from in the søknad?

The søknad should focus on why you fit the job. If your background is relevant, for example experience from your home country or language skills the employer needs, mention it briefly and connect it to the job. Keep personal details short and factual.
