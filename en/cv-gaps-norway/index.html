CV Gaps in Norway: How to Explain Them Briefly and Honestly

Published November 2, 2026

Almost everyone has a gap in their CV at some point. You may have moved to Norway and spent months settling in, taken a Norwegian course, stayed home with a child, been ill or simply looked for work longer than you hoped. A gap on its own rarely ruins an application. What matters is how you explain it. This article shows you how to do that in your CV, your søknad (job application) and the interview, with wording you can adapt.

Why short and honest works best in Norway

Norwegian application culture is direct and modest. Employers usually prefer a plain, factual explanation to a long story or a clever way of hiding the gap. If dates are missing or blurred, the reader will often notice and wonder why.

A good explanation has three parts:

  1. What happened, in a few words.
  2. What you did during that time, if anything is relevant.
  3. Where you are now: ready to work and interested in this job.

You do not need to apologise, and you do not need to share private details. One honest sentence is usually enough.

Decide whether the gap needs explaining

Not every pause needs a comment. A few months between two jobs, or the summer after finishing a degree, is normal, and many readers will not think twice about it. Longer periods, or gaps right before the job you are applying for, are worth a short line.

A useful test: look at your CV as if you were the employer. If you would ask 'what happened here?', answer that question before anyone asks it.

Whatever you do, do not change dates to hide a gap. Employers may contact your referanser (references) or ask for certificates of employment, and wrong dates can cost you far more trust than the gap itself. If you have recently come back after a long break, our article on applying for a job in Norway after a long career break goes into more depth.

How to show a gap in your CV

Norwegian CVs are normally in reverse chronological order and one to two pages long. The simplest approach is to give the gap its own short line in the right place, just like a job.

Before (the gap is visible but unexplained):

  • 2023–present: Warehouse worker, Y AS, Drammen
  • 2019–2021: Logistics coordinator, X Company, Poland

After (the gap is explained in one line):

  • 2023–present: Warehouse worker, Y AS, Drammen
  • 2021–2023: Moved to Norway. Norwegian course (norskkurs), now at B1 level. Part-time volunteer at a local sports club.
  • 2019–2021: Logistics coordinator, X Company, Poland

Some tips:

  • Put anything useful you did into the line: courses, volunteering, freelance work, caring responsibilities or further education.
  • Keep the line factual. 'Parental leave' or 'Full-time carer for a family member' is enough.
  • If you took courses during the gap, you can also list them under Education or Courses.
  • Use the same dates in your CV, your søknad and any online profile, such as LinkedIn.

Example wording for common gaps

Here are short phrases you can adapt. Use only what is true for you.

Moving to Norway

2022–2023: Relocated to Norway with my family. Took a Norwegian course and settled in Trondheim.

Learning Norwegian

2023–2024: Full-time Norwegian course at Z adult education centre. Current level: B1, still improving through evening classes.

If you do not know your CEFR level, use plain words such as 'basic', 'conversational' or 'fluent', and say what you are doing to improve.

Parental leave or caring for family

2021–2023: Parental leave and full-time care for my two children.

Illness

2022: Period of illness. Now fully able to work.

Only write the second sentence if it is true. You do not have to name a diagnosis.

Looking for work

2024: Job seeking after relocating. Completed an online course in Excel and volunteered at a second-hand shop.

Further education

2020–2022: Full-time master's programme at Z University, with a thesis on local energy planning.

Travel or personal time

2019: Six months of travel in South America. Improved my Spanish.

Mentioning the gap in your søknad

Your søknad is usually no longer than one page, so the gap should take up one or two sentences at most. Mention it only if it is long or recent, and put it near the end, after you have shown why you fit the job. Lead with your strengths, not with the gap.

Too long and apologetic:

I am very sorry for the gap in my CV. As you can see, I have not worked since 2022, which has been a difficult time for me, and I fully understand if this is a concern...

Better:

After moving to Norway in 2022, I focused on learning Norwegian and now speak it at B1 level. I am ready to start full-time and look forward to using my logistics experience again.

The second version is honest, short and points forward. For more on structuring the whole letter, see what to write in a Norwegian cover letter, paragraph by paragraph.

Talking about the gap in the interview

If the interviewer asks about the gap, answer calmly in two or three sentences and then move on to the job. Prepare your answer in advance, and say it out loud a few times so it sounds natural.

Example from Ana, who stayed home with her children:

I was on parental leave for two years and took care of my children. During that time I kept up to date by taking an online accounting course. Now my children are in kindergarten, and I am ready to return to work full-time.

Example from Tomasz, who searched for work for a long time:

It took longer than I expected to find work after moving here, mainly because I was still learning Norwegian. I used the time to reach B1 and to volunteer, and I am now looking for a job where I can use my warehouse experience.

Avoid blaming former employers or giving long explanations. If the reason is private, a short answer is perfectly acceptable:

I had a period of illness that is now behind me, and I am fully ready to work.

Summary

  • A gap is common and rarely a problem on its own. An unclear or hidden gap is worse.
  • Explain it in one honest line in your CV, in reverse chronological order.
  • Include anything useful you did: Norwegian courses, volunteering, further education, caring.
  • In your søknad, mention it briefly and only after your strengths.
  • In the interview, answer in two or three sentences and turn to the job.
  • Never change dates, and keep them the same in every document.

Frequently asked questions

Should I leave the gap out of my CV completely?

It is usually better to show it. If years are simply missing, the employer may assume the worst or ask about it anyway. One short, factual line gives you control over how the gap is understood.

Do I have to tell the employer why I was ill?

No. A short phrase such as 'period of illness' is usually enough, followed by 'now fully able to work' if that is true. You can keep medical details private in your CV, søknad and interview.

Does a Norwegian language course count as something to put in my CV?

Yes. A Norwegian course shows effort and is relevant for many jobs in Norway, especially when the ad asks for Norwegian skills. Write your current level honestly, with a CEFR level if you know it, and mention if you are still taking classes.

I was unemployed for a long time after moving. How do I say that without sounding negative?

Say it plainly and add what you did in the meantime, such as learning Norwegian, volunteering or taking short courses. Then point forward to what you can offer now. A calm, factual tone usually comes across better than an apology.

Should I mention the gap in both my CV and my søknad?

Show it in your CV with one line. In the søknad, only mention it if the gap is long or recent, and keep it to one or two sentences near the end. There is no need to repeat everything in both documents.
