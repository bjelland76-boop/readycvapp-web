How to Read a Norwegian Job Ad (Stillingsannonse): Requirements, Wishes and What Lies Between the Lines

Published November 19, 2026

A Norwegian job ad, or stillingsannonse, can look short and friendly. Read it more carefully, though, and it contains a lot of information about what the employer needs, what they would simply like, and what the job is really like day to day. If you are applying in English, you may meet ads written in Norwegian, in English, or in a mix of both. This guide shows you how to read an ad on FINN.no, arbeidsplassen.nav.no or LinkedIn so you can decide whether to apply and what to focus on in your application.

The usual structure of a Norwegian job ad

Most Norwegian ads follow a similar pattern. Once you know the headings, you can scan an ad quickly:

  • Om oss / Om arbeidsgiveren (about us): who the employer is.
  • Arbeidsoppgaver (tasks): what you will actually do.
  • Kvalifikasjoner (qualifications): education, experience, certificates and language skills.
  • Personlige egenskaper (personal qualities): the kind of colleague they want.
  • Vi tilbyr (we offer): working conditions, team, training.
  • Søknadsfrist (application deadline), tiltredelse (start date) and kontaktperson (contact person).

Read the tasks section before the qualifications. The tasks tell you what the job really is, and they help you judge which qualifications matter most.

Requirements versus wishes: the most important distinction

Norwegian ads often separate what you must have from what would be nice to have. The wording tells you which is which.

Words that usually signal a real requirement:

  • krav or det er et krav (it is a requirement)
  • må ha (must have)
  • vi forutsetter (we expect / it is a condition)
  • relevant utdanning innen ... (relevant education in ...)

Words that usually signal a wish, not a requirement:

  • ønskelig or det er ønskelig med (desirable)
  • det er en fordel (it is an advantage)
  • gjerne (preferably)
  • erfaring fra ... vil bli vektlagt (experience in ... will count in your favour)

Here is an invented example from a warehouse job at Y AS:

Kvalifikasjoner: Førerkort klasse B er et krav. Truckførerbevis er en fordel. Erfaring fra lagerarbeid er ønskelig.

In plain English: a driving licence is essential, a forklift certificate is a plus, and warehouse experience is welcome but not essential. If you have the driving licence and have worked in a busy supermarket stockroom, this job is worth applying for, even without the forklift certificate.

A useful rule of thumb: if you meet all the requirements and some of the wishes, apply. If you are missing a clear requirement, such as an authorisation or a specific degree, think twice before applying.

How to read the Norwegian language requirement

Many ads mention Norwegian, even when the ad itself is written in English. Read the exact phrase, because the level varies a lot:

  • Du må beherske norsk muntlig og skriftlig – you must handle spoken and written Norwegian well. Expect daily use, including writing.
  • Gode norskkunnskaper – good Norwegian skills. Usually means you can work in Norwegian without help.
  • Norsk er en fordel – Norwegian is an advantage. English may be enough to start.
  • Arbeidsspråket er engelsk – the working language is English.

If the ad asks for Norwegian and you are still learning, do not hide it, and do not exaggerate. Describe your level honestly in your CV and application, for example:

Norwegian: B1 (conversational). I am taking an evening course and use Norwegian with colleagues every day.

If you do not know your CEFR level, plain words such as "basic", "conversational" or "fluent" work well. Employers tend to respect honesty plus a clear plan to improve.

Reading between the lines

Some phrases in Norwegian ads tell you more than they seem to. Here are common ones and what they often suggest:

  • Selvstendig (independent): you may work alone or with little supervision. Good if you like responsibility.
  • Høyt tempo (fast pace): expect busy periods, pressure and many tasks at once.
  • Fleksibel (flexible): may mean varied hours, evenings or weekends, or changing tasks.
  • Turnus (shift rotation): common in care homes, hospitals and hotels. Check whether it includes nights and weekends.
  • Vi er i en spennende utviklingsfase (we are in an exciting phase of development): the company is changing or growing. Things may be less settled.
  • Løsningsorientert (solution-oriented): they want people who fix problems without waiting to be told.
  • Trives i et uformelt miljø (enjoys an informal environment): flat structure, first names, direct communication with managers. This is typical in Norway.

Also notice what the ad repeats. If "customer service" appears in the tasks, the qualifications and the personal qualities, it is clearly central to the job. Make it central in your application too.

A very long list of qualifications usually describes a dream candidate, not the minimum. Nobody ticks every box.

The practical details that are easy to miss

The small print at the end of an ad is often where important facts hide:

  • Fast stilling: a permanent position.
  • Vikariat or midlertidig stilling: a temporary position, for example covering someone's leave.
  • Heltid / deltid: full-time / part-time. Stillingsprosent tells you what share of a full-time position the job is.
  • Søknadsfrist: the application deadline. Snarest means as soon as possible.
  • Fortløpende: applications are reviewed as they arrive, so the job may be filled before the deadline. Apply early.
  • Politiattest: a police certificate. Some jobs, for example in care or with children, ask for one.
  • Kontaktperson: the named contact. Address your application to this person, and feel free to call with a short, relevant question before applying.

A good question to the contact person might be:

Hei Kari, I am interested in the warehouse position at Y AS. Could you tell me how much Norwegian is used on a typical shift?

A quick checklist before you decide to apply

When you have read the ad, go through these five questions:

  1. Do I meet every clear requirement (krav, må ha)?
  2. Which wishes (ønskelig, en fordel) can I show with real examples?
  3. Does my Norwegian level match the wording in the ad?
  4. Do the hours, contract type and start date work for me?
  5. Which two or three things does the ad repeat, so that I should highlight them?

Summary

Read the tasks first, then sort the qualifications into requirements and wishes. Pay close attention to the exact wording about Norwegian, and be honest about your level. Look for repeated themes and phrases that describe the working day, and check the practical details at the end, especially fortløpende and the contact person. A careful reading saves you from applying to the wrong jobs and helps you write a much stronger application for the right ones.

Frequently asked questions

Should I apply if I do not meet all the qualifications in a Norwegian job ad?

Often, yes. Look at the wording: things marked as ønskelig or en fordel are wishes, not requirements. If you meet the clear requirements and some of the wishes, it is usually worth applying. If you lack something marked as a krav, think carefully first.

The ad is in English but mentions Norwegian. Do I need to speak Norwegian?

It depends on the phrase. "Norwegian is an advantage" is different from "you must master spoken and written Norwegian". Read the exact wording, and if you are unsure, ask the contact person how much Norwegian is used in the job.

What does 'fortløpende' mean in a job ad?

It means the employer reviews applications and may hold interviews as they come in. The position can be filled before the søknadsfrist. Apply as early as you can when you see this word.

Is it okay to call the contact person before applying?

Yes, this is common in Norway when the ad gives a contact person. Keep the call short and ask a real question about the job, not something already answered in the ad. You can mention the conversation briefly in your application.

Where can I find Norwegian job ads?

The main places are FINN.no, arbeidsplassen.nav.no (run by NAV) and LinkedIn. Many employers also post vacancies on their own websites. Reading several ads for the same type of job helps you learn the typical wording quickly.
